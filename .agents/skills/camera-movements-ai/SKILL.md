## 💡 Diretrizes de Engenharia de Prompt (Director's Eye - Pro Tips)

1. **Posição no Prompt:** Coloque a instrução técnica de câmera e iluminação no **início do prompt** ou no bloco visual principal.
2. **Especificidade Numérica e Termos Fortes:**
   - Para *Dutch Angle*: Use `DUTCH ANGLE, camera tilted 25 degrees clockwise, diagonal horizon line`.
   - Para *Bird's Eye*: Use `TRUE BIRD'S EYE VIEW, camera directly overhead pointing straight down at 90 degrees, top of head visible`.
   - Para *Medium Shot*: Especifique a linha de corte: `STRICT MEDIUM SHOT, framed precisely from waist up`.
3. **Associação Tripla (Câmera + Lente + Luz):** Para máxima estética de cinema, combine 1 técnica de cada categoria. Exemplo:
   `Cinematic tracking shot, anamorphic widescreen 2.39:1, volumetric god rays through fog...`

## 📐 Categoria 1: Ângulos de Câmera e Enquadramentos (Camera Angles & Framing)

1. **High Angle Shot**: `EXTREME HIGH ANGLE SHOT — camera positioned 12 feet HIGH ABOVE the subject, tilted DOWN at the subject at a steep 60 degree angle. We see the TOP of the subject's head and the subject's shoulders in clear forced perspective. The subject looks UP toward the camera. The subject's body appears compressed and small from above. The ground occupies the lower 70% of the frame, sky is barely visible. We are clearly looking DOWN ON the subject — NOT at eye level, NOT a regular front shot. The high vantage point is the entire visual point.` — *Camera positioned above the subject, looking down. Provides a unique perspective and can make the subject appear smaller within their environment.*
2. **Low Angle Shot**: `EXTREME LOW ANGLE SHOT — camera placed on the GROUND at the subject's feet, tilted UP at the subject at a steep 60 degree angle. We look STEEPLY UP toward the subject's face. The subject towers above the camera, dominating the frame. The sky and tall architecture (red pillars, pagoda spire) fill the upper portion. Her chin and the underside of the subject jaw are visible. The subject is the tallest thing in the frame, dwarfing us. NOT eye level, NOT a regular shot — clearly we are LOOKING UP from below.` — *Camera positioned below the subject, looking up. Makes the subject appear powerful, heroic, or imposing.*
3. **Dutch Angle**: `DUTCH ANGLE / CANTED FRAME — camera dramatically TILTED on its roll axis by exactly 25 degrees clockwise. The HORIZON LINE and all vertical elements (rooftops, walls, trees) are visibly DIAGONAL across the frame, NOT level. The entire image is rotated. The subject stands within the tilted frame. Visual tension and unease come from the broken horizon. NOT a level shot — the frame is clearly canted, like a ship listing in storm. Vertical building lines lean diagonally.` — *Camera tilted on its roll axis, creating a diagonal horizon line. Creates visual tension, unease, or psychological tension.*
4. **Bird's Eye View**: `TRUE BIRD'S EYE VIEW — camera positioned DIRECTLY OVERHEAD, pointing STRAIGHT DOWN at 90 degrees vertical. We see ONLY the TOP of the subject's head (her copper hair flowing outward) and the tops of her shoulders. NO face visible. NO frontal body visible. The ground — stone courtyard patterns, cherry blossom petals scattered on flagstones — fills the ENTIRE FRAME around the subject. The subject appears small, centered, viewed from high above like a satellite image. This is a pure overhead top-down shot — NOT a high angle, NOT a tilted view. The camera is LITERALLY above her head pointing straight down.` — *Camera positioned directly overhead looking straight down at 90 degrees. Top-down perspective used for reveals and geometric compositions.*
5. **Extreme Close-Up**: `EXTREME CLOSE-UP — tight macro frame on the subject eye. Every detail visible: warm brown iris, individual eyelashes, subtle eye reflections of pink cherry blossoms. Razor sharp, shallow depth of field.` — *Tight framing on a specific detail — eyes, lips, hands. Creates intimacy and emphasizes emotion.*
6. **Long Shot**: `LONG SHOT — the subject full body visible, walking gracefully through the wide Chinese courtyard. The architecture stretches around the subject with empty space. The subject is identifiable but small in the grand setting. Wide cinematic framing.` — *Shows the subject's full body within their environment. Establishes spatial relationships and character within their world.*
7. **Medium Shot**: `STRICT MEDIUM SHOT — the frame is cut EXACTLY at the subject's waist. Nothing below the waist is visible — NO hips, NO thighs, NO legs, NO feet. The bottom edge of the frame slices precisely across her belt line. The subject is framed from the WAIST UP to just above the head. Chest, shoulders, arms, and head fill the majority of the frame. This is tighter than a cowboy shot (which goes to mid-thigh) — the waist is the bottom cut line, strict and absolute. Hanok village blurred behind.` — *Frames the subject from roughly the waist up. Balances facial expression with body language.*
8. **Close-Up**: `CLOSE-UP — head and shoulders only, the subject's face fills most of the frame. Looking thoughtfully off to the side. Soft pink cherry blossom bokeh fills the background.` — *Tight framing on the subject's face. Captures subtle emotions and creates intimacy with the audience.*
9. **Worm's Eye View**: `WORM'S EYE VIEW — camera flat on the ground pointing up. The subject stands above looking down toward camera with curious expression. The pagoda and cherry blossoms tower into the sky behind the subject. Extreme foreshortening.` — *Camera at ground level pointing straight up. Creates dramatic vertigo effect and emphasizes scale.*
10. **Over the Shoulder Shot**: `OVER THE SHOULDER SHOT — camera behind the subject's left shoulder. We see the subject's hair and shoulder framing the left side, looking out at the Hanok village stretching ahead. The subject is in the foreground, the village is the focus.` — *Camera positioned behind one character's shoulder, showing what they see. Creates depth and perspective.*
11. **Extreme Wide Shot**: `EXTREME WIDE SHOT — the subject is a small figure in the vast Chinese courtyard. Massive pillars and pagoda dominate the frame. The environment is the main subject; the subject provides scale. Epic, cinematic establishing shot.` — *Very wide framing showing vast environment with subject very small. Establishes scale and place.*
12. **Back Shot**: `BACK SHOT — camera behind the subject. We see only the subject's hair and back of the subject's clothing as the subject gazes out at the Kyoto temple complex stretching ahead. The viewer experiences the scene through the subject perspective.` — *Camera behind the subject showing what they see. Creates mystery and invites the viewer into their perspective.*
13. **POV Shot**: `POV SHOT — first-person perspective, as if seen through the subject's eyes. Her hands visible at the bottom edge of the frame, holding a paper umbrella. The temple grounds stretch ahead — stone path, lanterns, the pagoda in the distance. Camera height matches the subject eye level. Subtle handheld feel. Immersive first-person view, the viewer IS the character.` — *Point of view shot — what the character sees through their own eyes. Immersive first-person perspective.*
14. **Establishing Shot**: `ESTABLISHING SHOT — extremely wide cinematic shot of the entire courtyard at golden hour. The subject stands tiny in the distance, dwarfed by the architecture. Foreground stone steps lead the eye into the scene. Multiple buildings, courtyards, and the distant pagoda all visible. Sweeping panoramic feel. The scene is the subject — she's a small but anchoring presence.` — *Wide opening shot that introduces a location and sets the scene before more intimate framing follows.*
15. **Aerial Shot**: `AERIAL DRONE SHOT — captured from far above the village, looking down at a steep angle. Curved tile rooftops form geometric patterns below. The subject is a small figure walking through a narrow stone alley between houses. Bird's-eye perspective with strong sense of height. Drone photography aesthetic, soft afternoon light.` — *Drone or helicopter perspective — high above the scene, revealing scale and spatial relationships from the sky.*
16. **Medium Close-Up**: `MEDIUM CLOSE-UP — framing from the subject's chest to the top of the subject's head. The subject faces the camera with a soft, neutral expression. Cherry blossoms blurred behind the subject. Conversational distance — close enough for emotion, wide enough to include shoulders and gesture. Classic dialogue framing.` — *Frames the subject from chest to top of head — between a close-up and medium shot. The dialogue workhorse.*
17. **Cowboy Shot**: `COWBOY SHOT — framing from the subject mid-thigh to the top of the subject's head. The subject stands relaxed, hands at the subject's sides, looking off to the side. The Chinese courtyard stretches behind the subject. Western film framing — gives space to the body language and stance while keeping intimacy with the face.` — *Frames the subject from mid-thigh to top of head — the iconic Western framing that makes characters feel grounded and ready.*
18. **Eye-Level Shot**: `EYE-LEVEL SHOT — camera positioned at EXACTLY the subject's eye height, lens axis perfectly horizontal. NO tilt up, NO tilt down. The subject's eyes are aligned directly with the camera lens — a level-to-level connection. Neither looking down ON her nor looking up AT her. This is the neutral baseline camera angle. Medium close-up framing (chest to top of head). Direct natural eye contact. The feeling is conversational and equal, like standing face to face with her.` — *The neutral baseline angle — camera at exactly the subject's eye height, lens horizontal. Feels natural and direct.*
19. **Three-Quarter View**: `THREE-QUARTER VIEW PORTRAIT — the subject's body is turned 45 degrees AWAY from the camera (her shoulders at a 45-degree angle to the lens), while her face is rotated back toward the camera at a 3/4 angle. We can see BOTH the front of her face AND partial side of her face simultaneously. One eye appears slightly closer to camera, the other slightly farther. Her nose points between direct-to-camera and full profile. This is NOT a direct front-on (which would have symmetrical face) and NOT a profile (which would show only one side). Classic painter's 3/4 portrait pose. Medium close-up framing.` — *Subject's body turned 45 degrees from camera while face is angled back. The most common portrait pose.*
20. **Two-Shot**: `TWO-SHOT DIALOGUE FRAMING — the subject (the copper-haired girl from the reference) is framed together with A DIFFERENT SECOND PERSON beside her — a second young woman with straight dark brown hair in a plain neutral beige cardigan. The second woman is explicitly NOT the same person as the reference — different face, different hair color, different features. The two women stand side by side, both framed from chest up, both heads at similar height in the frame. The subject is on the LEFT, the second figure on the RIGHT. They face slightly toward each other mid-conversation. Both evenly lit. Classic dialogue two-shot. Hanok village blurred behind.` — *Two subjects framed together in the same shot. The standard framing for dialogue and relationship scenes.*
21. **Profile Shot**: `STRICT PROFILE SHOT — the subject is facing EXACTLY 90 degrees sideways to the camera. We see her LEFT-SIDE PROFILE ONLY: the clean silhouette of her forehead, nose, lips, chin, and jawline against the blurred background. ONE eye visible from the side (her left eye). Her body is also turned sideways. We see the shape of her face as a pure side silhouette. This is NOT 3/4, NOT front-on — it is a strict side view. Medium close-up framing showing her head and upper shoulders from the side. The outline of her profile is the entire point of the composition.` — *Subject facing exactly sideways — pure silhouette of the face in profile. Strong graphic portrait style.*

## 🏃 Categoria 2: Movimentos de Câmera em Vídeo (Camera Movements)

22. **Push In (Dolly In)**: `DOLLY PUSH-IN: The subject is FROZEN IN PLACE — feet planted, body completely motionless, NOT walking, NOT moving forward. ONLY the camera physically moves through 3D space toward the subject's face on a dolly track. The camera approaches the subject from a distance, getting closer and closer to the subject's face, while the subject stays at the exact same position in the world. As the camera advances, the subject's face fills more of the frame because the camera is closer, NOT because the subject walks toward it. Smooth mechanical dolly motion. The background stays in place behind the subject — only the perspective changes.` — *Camera slowly moves toward the subject, creating intimacy and intensifying focus on emotion or revelation.*
23. **Pull Out (Dolly Out)**: `Camera slowly pulls OUT, away from the subject, revealing more of the Chinese imperial courtyard. The subject stays still in the center as the scene grows around the subject. Gradual reveal. Smooth dolly-out. 4 seconds.` — *Camera slowly moves away from the subject, revealing the environment and creating a sense of isolation or scale.*
24. **Pan Left**: `Camera pans smoothly to the LEFT, revealing more of the Chinese courtyard. The subject stays in frame as new architecture comes into view. Steady horizontal rotation. 4 seconds.` — *Camera rotates horizontally on its axis, sweeping the view left. Reveals environment or follows action.*
25. **Tilt Up**: `Camera starts at the subject's feet, then TILTS UP slowly to reveal the subject standing tall, then continues up to the cherry blossoms and pagoda above. Vertical reveal. 4 seconds.` — *Camera rotates vertically upward. Reveals scale, height, or surprise.*
26. **Tilt Down**: `Camera starts above looking at the pagoda, then TILTS DOWN slowly to reveal the subject standing in the courtyard below. Smooth vertical descent. 4 seconds.` — *Camera rotates vertically downward. Often used for dramatic reveals.*
27. **Side Tracking Shot**: `The subject walks forward through the bamboo forest path. Camera tracks alongside the subject at the same speed, maintaining a side profile. Bamboo stalks blur past in the background. Smooth lateral movement. 4 seconds.` — *Camera moves alongside the subject as they walk, maintaining the same distance. Creates a sense of journey.*
28. **Forward Tracking**: `The subject walks slowly forward through the Kyoto temple complex. Camera follows behind the subject at the same pace, showing the subject's back and the temple stretching ahead. Immersive forward motion. 4 seconds.` — *Camera follows behind the subject as they walk forward. Immersive POV-adjacent experience.*
29. **Orbit Shot**: `CIRCULAR TRACKING SHOT around a frozen statue. The subject is COMPLETELY MOTIONLESS — not turning, not pivoting, body locked in one direction facing one way. The camera takes a curved walking path AROUND the subject in a horizontal arc. Shot 1: viewpoint sees the subject from the front. Then viewpoint moves to see the subject from the right side (3/4 view). Then viewpoint moves to see the subject from behind the subject. Then viewpoint moves to see the subject from the left side. The subject's body never rotates — only the camera's POSITION around the subject changes, revealing different angles of the same frozen pose. Background scenery continuously rotates as the camera changes position. Think: walking around a sculpture in a museum.` — *Camera circles around the subject, who remains the focal point. Creates dynamic, dramatic emphasis.*
30. **Crane Up (Boom Up)**: `VERTICAL CAMERA RISE through 3D space — the camera physically RISES UPWARD, gaining altitude like a hot air balloon ascending. NOT a tilt (where camera rotates), NOT a pan. The camera's POSITION in the air gets HIGHER over time. Starting position: camera at the subject's eye level (5 feet off the ground). Ending position: camera 30 feet in the air, looking down at a steeper angle. As the camera rises, the viewer sees the subject getting smaller below, more rooftops become visible, the horizon expands. Vertical altitude change is the key motion. The subject stays frozen on the ground. This is camera gaining HEIGHT, not rotating its lens.` — *Camera rises vertically, revealing more of the environment from above. Often used for dramatic reveals.*
31. **Dolly Zoom (Vertigo Effect)**: `DOLLY ZOOM (Vertigo Effect) — The subject stands frozen in place at the same size in frame throughout the entire shot, body motionless, expression unchanged, identity unchanged (same exact face, hair, clothing). Behind the subject, the BACKGROUND undergoes a dramatic perspective warp: the trees, towers, and distant elements visibly stretch, expand, and distort — appearing to recede away from the subject while simultaneously growing larger. This creates the disorienting vertigo effect where the subject stays fixed but the background space warps unnaturally. Smooth continuous warp, no cuts, no character changes. The character is a stable anchor while the world bends around the subject.` — *Camera moves toward subject while zooming out (or vice versa). Background appears to stretch dramatically while subject stays the same size. Creates psychological disorientation.*
32. **Whip Pan**: `ONE CONTINUOUS UNBROKEN SHOT — NO CUT, NO CROSSFADE, NO RETURN TO SUBJECT. Frame 1 (1 sec): subject visible on the left side of frame, body completely still. Then the camera SUDDENLY swings rapidly to the RIGHT, creating massive horizontal motion blur for 1 second — the entire frame becomes streaks of color. After the rapid swing settles, the camera ENDS pointed at a DIFFERENT part of the courtyard showing a hanging paper lantern. The subject does NOT reappear — the shot ends on the lantern. NO morph back to the subject, NO crossfade, the camera physically swung away and stays away. Single take with motion-blurred whip transition mid-shot.` — *Extremely fast horizontal pan that creates a motion blur transition. Energetic and dynamic.*
33. **Handheld Shot**: `The subject walks forward through the bamboo forest. Camera follows with subtle natural HANDHELD shake — slight bounces, micro-adjustments. Documentary realism. 4 seconds.` — *Camera held by hand, creating natural shake and movement. Adds urgency, realism, or intimacy.*
34. **Steadicam Glide**: `The subject walks gracefully through the Hanok village. Camera glides smoothly behind and slightly above the subject, no shake at all. Floating, weightless movement following the subject path. 4 seconds.` — *Smooth flowing camera movement that follows the subject without shake. Cinematic and professional.*
35. **Rack Focus (Video)**: `Camera holds steady. Focus starts on a single cherry blossom in the foreground, then RACKS smoothly to the subject's face in the background. Focus pull from bloom to woman. Attention shift. 4 seconds.` — *Focus shifts from one element to another within the same shot, redirecting viewer attention.*
36. **Zoom In**: `Camera position is fixed but ZOOMS IN on the subject smoothly with a telephoto lens. The Chinese courtyard compresses behind the subject as the focal length increases. 4 seconds.` — *Lens zooms toward subject while camera stays still. Different feel than dolly-in — flatter, more clinical.*
37. **Zoom Out**: `ZOOM OUT (telephoto pull-back) — Starting frame: the subject's face fills most of the frame in close-up. The CAMERA stays in the same physical position throughout, but the LENS zooms outward (focal length decreases continuously). As the lens zooms out, more and more of the surrounding environment is revealed around the subject — first the subject's shoulders, then the subject's body, then the trees, then the wider scene. The subject remains COMPLETELY MOTIONLESS throughout, identity unchanged (same face, same hair, same clothing — NOT a different person, NOT a morph). Continuous single take, no cuts, no transitions, no crossfade between scenes. Smooth continuous lens zoom from tight close-up to wide reveal.` — *Lens zooms away from subject while camera stays still. Reveals environment.*
38. **Vertical Pan**: `Camera tilts upward, sweeping from the subject's feet up the towering bamboo stalks toward the canopy. Reveals the height of the forest above. Vertical reveal. 4 seconds.` — *Vertical sweep showing height and verticality of a scene.*

## 💡 Categoria 3: Iluminação Cinematográfica (Cinematic Lighting Styles)

39. **Chiaroscuro Lighting**: `CHIAROSCURO LIGHTING — EXTREME high-contrast lighting in the style of Caravaggio paintings. Single hard light source from the left creates a brilliant slash of light across one side of the subject's face while the otthe subject's side falls into ABSOLUTE BLACK shadow. The background is mostly engulfed in deep darkness with only small areas of light catching architectural details. The contrast ratio is extreme: 90% of the frame is deep shadow, 10% is brightly lit. Painterly, dramatic, baroque. NOT a normal portrait — a tenebrist masterpiece with crushed blacks and bright highlights.` — *Extreme contrast between light and dark, inspired by Renaissance painters like Caravaggio. Creates dramatic, painterly atmosphere.*
40. **Rim Lighting**: `RIM LIGHTING — strong warm sunset light directly behind the subject creates a bright glowing halo around the subject's hair and shoulders. The subject's face mostly in soft shadow. Kyoto pagoda silhouetted behind. Magical golden hour.` — *Light source positioned behind the subject creates a bright outline around their silhouette. Separates subject from background dramatically.*
41. **Silhouette**: `TRUE BACKLIT SILHOUETTE — the subject stands in front of a brilliant glowing sunset/sunrise sky with a strong sun directly behind the subject. The subject is rendered as a PURE BLACK SHAPE — totally featureless, NO skin tone visible, NO hair color visible (her copper hair is only a dark outline glow), NO clothing details, NO facial features. Her entire body is solid silhouette black. The bright sky and sun behind the subject are exposed for, making the subject's body underexposed to pure shadow. The only thing visible of the subject is the subject crisp outline against the bright background. Classic backlit silhouette photography. The contrast is extreme: blown-out bright sky + pitch black character.` — *Subject appears as a dark shape against a bright background. Eliminates detail, emphasizes form and posture.*
42. **Golden Hour**: `GOLDEN HOUR — bathed in warm orange-amber sunset light streaming across the Chinese courtyard. Her copper hair glows. Soft warm shadows. Long stretching shadows on the stone tiles. Lens flare.` — *Warm soft directional light during the hour after sunrise or before sunset. Creates warm, romantic, nostalgic atmosphere.*
43. **Volumetric Lighting**: `VOLUMETRIC LIGHTING — morning mist fills the bamboo forest. Dramatic god rays of sunlight cut through the canopy and haze, creating visible light beams around the subject. The subject stands in a shaft of golden light, dust particles dancing. Magical, ethereal.` — *Visible beams of light cutting through fog, mist, or haze. Creates depth, atmosphere, and a sense of the divine.*
44. **Split Lighting**: `SPLIT LIGHTING — exactly half the subject's face is in warm sunlight from the left, the other half in deep shadow. Sharp vertical divide down the subject's nose. Red lanterns glow in the dark side. Mysterious duality.` — *Light illuminates exactly half the face while the other half is in shadow. Creates duality, mystery, hidden nature.*
45. **Practical Lighting**: `PRACTICAL LIGHTING — at twilight, glowing red Chinese paper lanterns hang above the subject, casting warm red light on the subject's face and hair. Multiple lanterns at different distances create depth. The lanterns are visible in frame.` — *Light sources visible within the frame — lanterns, lamps, candles. Creates naturalistic, immersive lighting.*
46. **Butterfly Lighting**: `BUTTERFLY LIGHTING CLOSE-UP PORTRAIT — TIGHT HEAD-AND-SHOULDERS CROP, her face fills 65% of the frame, no body below the shoulders visible. A single HARD KEY LIGHT is positioned directly above and in front of her, angled steeply DOWN at 45 degrees. This creates a distinct SYMMETRIC BUTTERFLY-SHAPED SHADOW directly under her nose — a small dark shape centered under both nostrils like butterfly wings. Small soft shadows also fall under her cheekbones and under her chin. No fill light, no rim light. Background is dark and heavily blurred. The butterfly nose shadow is the entire visual point — crisp, centered, unmistakable. Glamour / Paramount / 1930s Hollywood style.` — *Key light positioned above and in front of the subject, creating a butterfly-shaped shadow under the nose. Classic glamour portrait style.*
47. **Loop Lighting**: `LOOP LIGHTING CLOSE-UP PORTRAIT — TIGHT HEAD-AND-SHOULDERS CROP, face fills 65% of the frame. A key light is positioned at 45 degrees horizontally and slightly above her eye line. This creates a distinct small LOOP-SHAPED NOSE SHADOW on her cheek — a narrow elongated shadow cast from her nose onto the cheek, but the shadow does NOT touch the shadow-side of her face (this is what separates loop from Rembrandt). The shadow forms a small clear 'loop' on one cheek. The other cheek is in gentle shadow. Background is dark and blurred. Close tight portrait framing — we only see her face and top of her shoulders.` — *Key light at 45 degrees creates a small loop-shaped shadow from the nose onto the cheek. The most common portrait lighting.*
48. **Rembrandt Lighting**: `REMBRANDT LIGHTING CLOSE-UP PORTRAIT — TIGHT HEAD-AND-SHOULDERS CROP, face fills 65% of the frame. A key light at 45 degrees creates the SIGNATURE REMBRANDT TRIANGLE: a small bright triangle of illuminated skin on the subject's SHADOW-SIDE CHEEK, framed above by the eye socket and below by the nose shadow. One side of her face is fully lit, the other side mostly in deep shadow EXCEPT for the small illuminated triangle on the cheek just below the eye. This triangle is unmistakable, bright, and shaped like a small right triangle with its bottom edge at the nose-line. Dark painterly chiaroscuro background. Dutch master portrait quality.` — *Defined by a small illuminated triangle of light on the shadow side of the face, just below the eye. Dramatic painterly portrait lighting.*
49. **High Key Lighting**: `HIGH KEY LIGHTING — bright, soft, even illumination. Almost no shadows on the subject's face. Light, airy, optimistic atmosphere. Everything feels bright and cheerful. The Chinese architecture glows in soft warm light.` — *Bright, even lighting with minimal shadows. Creates upbeat, cheerful, optimistic mood.*
50. **Low Key Lighting**: `LOW KEY LIGHTING — dramatic single light source with deep shadows everywhere else. Most of the subject's face and the Hanok background fall into darkness. Only key features are illuminated. Moody, mysterious noir atmosphere.` — *Dramatic lighting with deep shadows and high contrast. Creates moody, mysterious, or noir atmosphere.*
51. **Blue Hour**: `BLUE HOUR — twilight just after sunset. The sky is a deep blue-purple gradient. The Kyoto temple is silhouetted. Cool blue ambient light bathes the subject's face. Magical, contemplative mood. Subtle warm temple lights glow.` — *Period just after sunset or before sunrise when the sky takes on a deep blue color. Cool, magical, contemplative.*
52. **Neon Color Pop**: `NEON COLOR POP — vibrant red and gold light from Chinese lanterns spills across the subject's face creating bold color contrast. Magenta and amber tones split the scene. Modern editorial vibe with traditional Asian color palette.` — *Vivid colored lighting (neon, gels) creates strong color contrast and modern aesthetic.*
53. **Back Lit With Flare**: `BACK LIT WITH FLARE — sun directly behind the subject's head creates a brilliant rim light on the subject's hair and dramatic anamorphic lens flares streaking across the frame. Cherry blossoms glow in the backlight. Dreamy ethereal mood.` — *Sun directly behind subject creates lens flares, hair glow, and dreamy bokeh.*
54. **Broad Lighting**: `BROAD LIGHTING — the subject turns the subject's face slightly to one side. The light illuminates the side of the subject's face that's TURNED TOWARD the camera. The shadowed side is away from the viewer. Open, friendly portrait lighting.` — *Light falls on the side of the face that's turned toward the camera. Makes the face appear wider and more open.*
55. **Short Lighting**: `SHORT LIGHTING — the subject turns the subject's face slightly. The light illuminates the side of the subject's face that's TURNED AWAY from camera. The visible side is mostly in shadow. Slimming, dramatic, moody portrait.` — *Light falls on the side of the face turned away from the camera. Creates more shadows, slimming and dramatic effect.*
56. **Hard Light**: `HARD LIGHT — direct undiffused sunlight from a single angle, creating sharp-edged dark shadows on the subject's face and the ground. High contrast: bright highlights and deep blacks with no soft transition. Strong cast shadows from the subject's body and nearby objects. Crisp, edgy, dramatic. NOT softbox light — raw direct sun.` — *A direct, undiffused light source that creates sharp, high-contrast shadows. Dramatic and edgy.*
57. **Soft Light**: `SOFT LIGHT — diffused, wrapping light from an overcast sky or large window. No harsh shadows. Skin tones glow gently. Smooth tonal transitions from highlight to shadow. Beautiful, flattering, natural. Like a giant softbox in the sky. Studio beauty quality.` — *A diffused, gentle light source that wraps around the subject. Flattering and natural-looking.*
58. **Day for Night**: `DAY FOR NIGHT EFFECT — the entire scene color-graded heavy blue and underexposed to simulate moonlight, even though it was shot in daylight. Cool blue cast on everything, deep shadows, but enough detail to read the scene. The bamboo forest takes on an eerie nocturnal mood with cyan highlights. Classic Western film convention.` — *A classic film technique — shooting in daytime but color-grading to look like night. Iconic in Westerns and old cinema.*
59. **Dappled Light**: `DAPPLED LIGHT — sunlight filtered through dense bamboo leaves above creates a beautiful pattern of bright spots and shadow on the subject's face, body, and the path. Organic patches of warm light scattered across the scene. Natural, magical, mottled illumination. The light pattern is the entire visual signature.` — *Natural light filtered through leaves or branches, creating spots of light and shadow on the subject. Magical and organic.*
60. **Uplighting**: `UPLIGHTING — strong warm light shining upward at the subject's face from below. Shadows are inverted — they appear above the subject brows and under the subject's chin instead of below. Dramatic, theatrical, slightly unnatural. Like firelight from a campfire or footlights on a stage. Creates intense, slightly eerie visual mood.` — *Light shining upward at the subject, creating an unnatural, theatrical, sometimes sinister look.*
61. **Three-Point Lighting**: `CLASSIC THREE-POINT LIGHTING CLOSE-UP PORTRAIT — HEAD-AND-SHOULDERS CROP, face fills 60% of the frame. All three lights are clearly visible in their effect on the subject: (1) KEY LIGHT from 45 degrees creates the dominant shape and shadow on one side of her face (bright and clearly directional), (2) FILL LIGHT from the opposite side softens the shadow but does NOT eliminate it — the key-to-fill ratio is clearly asymmetric, one side noticeably brighter than the other, (3) RIM / BACK LIGHT from behind creates a clean bright outline separating her hair and shoulder edge from the dark background. The three-light effect is textbook and visible. Studio cinematic quality.` — *Classic lighting setup — key, fill, and back light working together. The foundation of cinema and studio portraiture.*
62. **Natural Light**: `NATURAL AVAILABLE LIGHT — the scene is lit ONLY by pure ambient daylight. No artificial lighting, no studio lights, no flash. The overhead sky is softly overcast creating a giant soft diffused light source. The light wraps gently around her face with soft subtle direction, enough to show form but with no harsh contrast. No dramatic shadows, no specular highlights. The overall feel is entirely natural and candid, as if the moment was grabbed during a real walk through the bamboo forest. Whatever light was available — that is the light in this image. Everyday, honest, documentary.` — *Pure available daylight, no artificial sources. The most natural, candid, documentary look.*
